# G1 — Architect seat · FanZuP v2 requirements · 2026-10-03
**Artifacts:** `specs/00-brief.md`, `specs/01-requirements.md` · **Upstream:** `gates/E1-enterprise-readiness.md`, repo at `a165610` (incl. `packages/shared/src/policy.ts`) · **Tier:** Enterprise
**Rubric:** coherence of the FR set; NFRs quantified and mutually compatible; nothing forces an architecture the team (solo founder + agents on Vercel/Render/Supabase) can't afford; full coverage of E1 blockers B1–B12 and conditions C1–C17.

**Seat verdict: REVISE (1 blocker).** The FR set is coherent: it reads as one product (Fund My Show, end to end, with its unhappy paths) plus a defensibly sequenced growth layer, not a grab-bag. Every E1 blocker maps to at least one requirement. The problems are (a) one family of P0 controls that the stated team physically can't operate, (b) several NFR pairs that contradict each other and will be found only in design or in production, and (c) condition coverage, which has no trace table and two real gaps.

## Findings
| Sev | Finding | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| [BLOCKER] | **P0 controls need two or more staff, and the brief says there is one.** Two different staff members are required to suspend a campaign, lift a suspension, approve refunds and releases above a threshold, grant finance/admin roles, and change ledger/audit migrations. The on-call rota needs a primary and a backup. FR-ID-003 also bars anyone "linked to" a campaign from reviewing it. The team is "solo founder with a day job, plus Claude agents" (brief §Constraints), and agents can't be a second human approver for a custodian's due diligence. As written, the beta can't legally suspend a fraudulent campaign, or the controls get bypassed in practice, which is worse than not specifying them. **Resolve before pass:** add a requirement that defines the minimum staffing model (e.g. a named second approver, such as a contracted ops/finance person or counsel), plus a break-glass path for a single approver with mandatory after-the-fact review, time-boxed to emergencies such as suspend-to-protect-fans. Then make each dual-control AC reference it. | FR-CMP-007; FR-ID-003; FR-DSP-001; NFR-OPS-07; NFR-OPS-09; NFR-COMP-06/17; brief §Constraints |
| [CONCERN] | **Immutable audit vs. deletion conflict.** NFR-COMP-11 puts IP, user agent and before/after images (which will include names, emails and addresses) into audit events. NFR-COMP-12 makes those rows undeletable and exports them to object-locked storage for 7 years or more. FR-PRV-003 and NFR-COMP-13 promise that deletion pseudonymises personal data. Both can't be true unless audit payloads carry tokenised references rather than raw PII. That is a schema-level decision; if it's found after audit tables exist, it means rework of append-only tables. **Condition:** add an NFR that audit/ledger payloads store PII only by reference to a deletable or crypto-shreddable store, or state the legal-hold exemption explicitly in FR-PRV-003. | NFR-COMP-11/12/13; FR-PRV-003; E1 C8/C9 |
| [CONCERN] | **Address purge window is shorter than the dispute window.** NFR-SEC-08 purges shipping addresses 90 days after delivery. FR-DSP-002 requires staff to attach delivery proof to chargeback cases, and card-network dispute windows for "not received / not as described" run to 120 days from the transaction or expected delivery date, sometimes longer. Purging first destroys the evidence that wins the dispute. **Condition:** tie the purge to "the later of N days after delivery or the close of the dispute window for that backing", or retain a redacted proof-of-delivery record. | NFR-SEC-08; FR-DSP-002; FR-FUL-002 |
| [CONCERN] | **Priority inversion and a parallel-counter contradiction.** FR-BCK-005 (P0) shows each backing's "money state (FR-PAY-009)", but FR-PAY-009 is P1. FR-PAY-009 also keeps public counters and has "a nightly check alert[ing] on any mismatch". That presumes counters stored separately from the ledger, which E1 C5 explicitly rejected ("counters derived from the ledger, not parallel"). **Condition:** raise the per-backing money-state derivation (FR-PAY-009 AC 1) to P0, and restate the counter AC as "derived from the ledger, or a cache rebuilt from it", with the nightly check as a backstop, not the mechanism. | FR-BCK-005; FR-PAY-009; E1 C5 |
| [CONCERN] | **Conditions C1–C17 have no traceability, and two are missing or partial.** The trace table covers blockers only. Walking the conditions: C1→FR-PLT-001, C2→FR-ID-005, C3→NFR-SEC-02, C4→FR-PAY-002 (partly: "provider-agnostic inbound events" isn't stated), C6→NFR-OPS-05, C8→NFR-COMP-11/12, C9→FR-PRV-002/003 and FR-ID-006, C10→NFR-SEC-08, C11→NFR-SEC-09 and NFR-OPS-07, C12→NFR-A11Y, C13→NFR-QA-02/03, C14→FR-GRO-001, C15→NFR-QA-05, C16→FR-ID-004, C17→FR-PLT-003 and NFR-I18N-01 are all covered. **C7 is not:** there's no discovery or search FR at all (NFR-PERF-06 constrains a list that no FR defines), and fans can't find a campaign except by direct link. **C5** is contradicted (previous finding). **Condition:** add a "conditions → requirements" table and an FR-CMP/FR-GRO discovery requirement. | Traceability table; E1 C4/C5/C7; NFR-PERF-06 |
| [CONCERN] | **B1 and B2 are only partly closed.** B1's resolution includes "abandoned checkouts expire and their payment intents are cancelled", but FR-PAY-001 only expires checkouts for perks *with a quantity limit*. Open intents on unlimited perks and no-reward pledges are left to FR-PAY-003's after-the-fact refund, and FR-TAX-004's cap only limits how many there are. B2 names "a fan cancelling during the live window", but FR-BCK-003 is P1 and isn't in the B2 trace row, so a real-money beta would have no self-serve cancel. **Condition:** generalise the expiry AC to every checkout, and either raise FR-BCK-003's cancel AC to P0 or record that support refunds (FR-DSP-001) are the beta remedy. | FR-PAY-001; FR-BCK-003; FR-TAX-004; traceability B1/B2 |
| [CONCERN] | **SLOs and on-call are sized for a team, not this one.** A 99.9% 28-day SLO (≈40 min/month) on checkout and public reads, RTO ≤ 4 h, "10 min heartbeat miss pages on-call", and SEV1/2 posting within 30 minutes all assume someone awake and available. The stack is Vercel + Supabase + a single Render worker (the E1 Pragmatist notes a $7 instance), and a provider outage alone can consume the error budget. The 50 backings/s figure is a GA assumption that's still unconfirmed (Q4), but it's written as P0. **Condition:** split Beta and GA objectives (e.g. Beta 99.5%, business-hours response, with money safety guaranteed by the fail-safe design rather than human response time), and gate the 50/s load target on Q4 at G2. | NFR-PERF-01/02; NFR-OPS-02/06/09/10; brief §Users (volume), Q4 |
| [CONCERN] | **Requirements force partner capabilities that don't exist yet.** FR-PAY-007 needs per-campaign balances and movements from the custodian by 10:00 ET daily. FR-ID-004 needs hosted identity *and* payout onboarding from "the payment or custody provider". FR-TAX-003 re-screens *all users* (100k backers/year at GA) whenever lists update. These are selection criteria for a custodian, KYC and screening vendor that haven't been chosen (E1 card B, 6–12 months), and screening every backer per list update is a per-record cost line. **Condition:** move these into an explicit "partner capability requirements" list that feeds vendor selection, and scope backer screening to what the processor already does unless counsel says otherwise. | FR-PAY-007; FR-ID-004; FR-TAX-003; brief §Constraints (external gates) |
| [CONCERN] | **Vendor and infrastructure footprint isn't costed.** The P0 set implies, on top of the kept stack: processor, custodian, identity, tax/TIN, sanctions, transactional email, error tracking/metrics, bot challenge, a shared rate-limit store (FR-TAX-004 "across server instances"), object-locked storage in a second account and region (NFR-OPS-03, NFR-COMP-12), paging, and SBOM/CodeQL tooling. That's roughly 10 or more vendors for a solo operator. None of them is unreasonable alone, but no requirement says "use Postgres where it suffices" (for example, the rate-limit store and the job queue in NFR-OPS-05). **Condition:** the design (G2) must carry a monthly cost and vendor list, and prefer the existing Supabase/Render footprint for the queue, rate limits and flags. | FR-TAX-004; NFR-OPS-03/04/05; NFR-COMP-12; FR-PLT-001 |
| [NIT] | **Crawler metadata plus edge caching implies server rendering on a Vite SPA.** FR-GRO-001 ("crawlers receive the metadata without running JavaScript", with live progress in images) and NFR-PERF-02 (edge-cacheable with ≤ 60 s staleness) together require an edge or SSR layer that the current React/Vite app doesn't have. This is affordable on Vercel, but it's a new runtime and should be named in 02-design rather than discovered. | FR-GRO-001; NFR-PERF-02; brief §Constraints (stack) |
| [NIT] | **Many "policy" values don't exist in `policy.ts`, and one contradicts it.** These are cited but absent: checkout hold window, refund second-approval threshold, release dual-approval threshold, dispute-ratio threshold, holdback, rate limits, max unconfirmed checkouts, attribution window and fan-problem SLA. FR-CMP-001 says milestone tranches are "optional", but `POLICY.campaign.minTranches = 2`. Add the missing keys and resolve the tranche contradiction before G2. | FR-PAY-001; FR-DSP-001/004/005; FR-ID-003; FR-TAX-004; FR-GRO-003; FR-CMP-001 |
| [NIT] | **The 1-hour and 1-business-day refund targets disagree.** FR-PAY-003 initiates refunds of unapplicable captures "within 1 hour", while NFR-COMP-05 says "within 1 business day". Pick one; the summary NFR should defer to the FR. | FR-PAY-003; NFR-COMP-05 |
