# E1 Council Review: ARCHITECT seat

Lens: coherence and structure, boundaries, scale, enterprise architecture gaps.

## Findings

| Sev | Finding | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| BLOCKER | **Money can be captured by the processor and never recorded in the ledger.** `record_backing_captured` raises if the campaign has passed `ends_at` or the perk `claimed` check fails. But the perk sold-out check at backing time reads `claimed`, which only moves on capture, so concurrent checkouts can oversell. A card confirmed seconds before the deadline also reaches the webhook after `settle_campaign` has run. In both cases the charge succeeds, the SQL raises, the webhook returns 500, Stripe retries forever, and nothing records the payment. There is no suspense account, no auto-refund path and no reconciliation job. At 10x checkout concurrency (drops and countdown finishes) this happens routinely. | `ledger_and_money.sql:169,172` (record_backing_captured); `routes/backings.ts:39`; `routes/webhooks.ts:20-28`; `money.ts:24-31` (settles regardless of in-flight `pending_payment`) |
| BLOCKER | **Milestone tranches 2+ can never be paid out.** Releases run only from the `campaign.funded` outbox event, which pays tranche 1 and stops at the first unverified tranche. `verify_tranche` writes no outbox event, and no API route or job calls it or `releaseVerifiedTranches` afterwards. Milestone funds stay in escrow and the campaign never leaves `funded`. That breaks the "milestone tranche releases" promise of Fund My Show. Tranche state changes need to be event-driven: verify → outbox `tranche.verified` → release. | `ledger_and_money.sql:262-269` (verify_tranche); `money.ts:35-60, 96-98`; no route in `apps/api/src/routes/*` |
| BLOCKER | **The backing create is not atomic across DB, partner and idempotency record.** The backing row is committed, then `createPayment` runs outside any transaction, then the idempotency row is written. If the partner call or the final write fails, the client retries with the same key, finds no idempotency row, and inserts a **second backing with a second partner payment** (key `payment:<newId>`). The orphan `pending_payment` rows never expire. Fix: reserve the idempotency key (status `in_progress`) in the same transaction as the backing insert, and derive the partner key from the client key, not the new row id. | `routes/backings.ts:21-26, 41-60`; `backings` has no expiry or sweeper (`core.sql:153`) |
| CONCERN | **The escrow boundary is only half abstracted.** `EscrowProvider` covers outbound calls only. Inbound confirmations (capture, refund, payout status) are a Stripe-typed webhook, `Deps` carries `stripe: Stripe \| null`, and `confirmsImmediately` branches money logic in the worker. So the claim "swap the implementation; nothing else changes" is false: a real partner (North Capital or an FBO bank) needs a new webhook route, event normalization, payout-confirmation handling (payouts are recorded as released before the partner confirms), and statement ingestion. Add `parseEvent()`/`handleEvent()` plus a provider-agnostic `escrow_events` inbox table with dedupe on the partner event id. | `escrow/provider.ts:6,16-26`; `app.ts:21`; `routes/webhooks.ts:1-45`; `money.ts:51-57,93`; `docs/SETUP.md` Part G |
| CONCERN | **The ledger has no reconciliation, and the counters run in parallel to it.** The migration says the ledger "must reconcile to the partner's statements", but there is no statement table, reconciliation job or break report. Meanwhile `campaigns.raised_minor`, `backers_count` and `perks.claimed` are hand-maintained counters, and `tranche_release_amount` mixes `raised_minor`, a sum over `backings` and a ledger balance. That gives three sources of truth for one number. | `ledger_and_money.sql:4-5,172-174,273-285` |
| CONCERN | **Outbox throughput and failure handling won't hold at 10x.** The drain is serial: one row per transaction, at most 25 rows per 30s tick (about 72k/day), on one FIFO with no topic priority. A failed campaign with 5k backers spends roughly 100 minutes on refunds, and every other campaign's tranche release queues behind it. Failures retry forever (backoff capped at 1h, no max attempts, no dead-letter, no alert), and an unknown topic poisons the queue silently. Each drain also holds an outbox row lock and a pooled connection open across a partner HTTP call, while `recordRefunded` and `record_tranche_released` take a second connection. That pattern exhausts the pool under parallel ticks. | `money.ts:66-84,92-93,101`; `worker.ts:14`; `ledger_and_money.sql:91-100,213-215`; `render.yaml` `DB_POOL_MAX=5` |
| CONCERN | **The ledger model is campaign-scoped and pooled, which blocks Layer 2 and per-user statements.** `backer_liability` is one account per campaign, so "what do we owe backer X" comes from `backings`, not the ledger. The check constraint and enum hard-code three campaign-scoped kinds. `ledger_account()` ignores `currency`, and so does the unique index. Balances are `sum()` over all entries with no snapshots, so global accounts like `processing_fees` get a full scan on every read. Reg CF offerings, investor holdings, distributions and 12-month issuer caps all need owner-scoped sub-accounts and an offering entity, not a campaign. | `ledger_and_money.sql:18-31,73-76,114-129,151-153`; `core.sql:91` |
| CONCERN | **The system has two write surfaces with no stated rule for which owns what.** Workflow transitions (`submit_campaign`, `review_campaign`, `publish_campaign`) and draft edits are client-callable through PostgREST (column grants plus RLS). Money goes through Fastify. Validation, rate limiting, audit actor, observability and versioning are therefore split across two front doors. Nothing on the web side uses either yet: 93 routes render `mock.ts`, and `api.ts` exposes 3 endpoints. Decide this before wiring: the API is the only write path, and PostgREST is read-only or off. Otherwise every enterprise concern gets solved twice. | `workflow_and_rls.sql:109-135`; `apps/web/src/lib/api.ts:6,44-56`; `apps/web/src/lib/mock.ts` |
| CONCERN | **The regulated feature flags exist only in the browser.** `layer2` and `postBeta` are read from Vite env or turned on by any visitor with `?flags=layer2` (persisted in sessionStorage). The API and DB know nothing about them. Once Layer 2 screens are wired, a URL parameter would expose Reg CF purchase flows. Flags need to be server-evaluated (per user, cohort and environment), enforced in the API and in RLS, and audited. | `apps/web/src/lib/flags.ts:21-43`; `apps/api/src/app.ts:50-56` (no flag gate) |
| CONCERN | **Discovery reads won't scale.** `GET /campaigns` returns every public campaign (including closed, failed and refunded ones) with no pagination, filtering or cache headers. It runs under RLS, which calls `owns_artist()` and `is_staff()` per row. There is no search index, no genre or city facets, and no CDN/edge caching of this anonymous read path. This is the first endpoint to fall over at 10x traffic. | `routes/campaigns.ts:21-24`; `workflow_and_rls.sql:166-167,205-210` |
| CONCERN | **There is no observability or operational backbone.** The worker logs ad-hoc `console.log` JSON. There are no request or correlation IDs threaded from HTTP to outbox to partner call, and no metrics (outbox lag, settle lag, webhook failures), tracing, alerting or worker heartbeat. The single Render starter instance is a silent single point of failure for settlement and refunds. Money incidents will be found by users before ops sees them. | `worker.ts:20,30`; `app.ts:27-43`; `render.yaml` (1 instance, no health check) |
| CONCERN | **Environments and disaster recovery are not designed.** Vercel preview deploys on every PR, but nothing defines which database a preview uses (no Supabase branching or seed isolation). There are no PITR/backup or restore targets (RPO/RTO) in SETUP or the ADRs. The append-only audit and ledger rely on triggers that the `postgres` owner can disable. With no off-database immutable export, the 6+ year retention promise (PRD 01 §10, cited in CR-001 FR-HV-006) depends on one Supabase project. | `docs/SETUP.md` Part B, D; `docs/adr/ADR-002-hosting.md`; `ledger_and_money.sql:68-71,79-88` |
| CONCERN | **The product surface has no schema behind it.** Tickets, merch orders, live streams and tips, subscriptions, notifications, disputes, payouts, KYC cases, Pools and holder votes (CR-001, now in scope) all exist as routes on mock data with no tables or bounded contexts. The only domain model is campaigns and backings. Enterprise build-out needs a domain map (Funding, Commerce, Live, Identity/Compliance, Securities, Comms) and module boundaries inside `apps/api` before these features land in one flat `routes/` folder. | `apps/web/src/app/routes.tsx:108-229`; `specs/backlog.md:5-12`; `specs/changes/CR-001-holder-votes.md` |
| NIT | `backers_count` goes up once per backing, not once per distinct backer, so a fan who backs twice counts as two backers on public cards. | `ledger_and_money.sql:174` |
| NIT | `api_idempotency` has no TTL or purge job (PRD 01a requires at least 30 days, not forever), and `audit_events` has no partitioning. Both grow without bound. | `ledger_and_money.sql:79-88,104-111` |
| NIT | `internal/tick` drains up to 50 partner calls in sequence inside one serverless invocation, which risks hitting function timeouts if it ever replaces the worker (ADR-002's stated migration path). | `routes/internal.ts:20-21`; `ADR-002` Consequences |

## Recommendations (enterprise capabilities, architect lens)
1. **Escrow event inbox and reconciliation service.** Normalize partner webhooks into `escrow_events` (deduped), run a daily reconciliation of ledger against partner statements with a break queue, and add a suspense account for late or orphaned captures. This makes the "FanZuP never holds cash" promise auditable.
2. **A durable job and event backbone** (pg-boss/Graphile Worker on Postgres now, or a managed queue later), with per-topic concurrency, max attempts, a dead-letter queue and replay. Refunds, releases, notifications and search indexing all depend on it.
3. **A notifications service** (email, push and in-app, with preferences and an E-SIGN consent ledger) fed from the outbox. Backer updates, refund notices, milestone proofs and CR-001 holder notices all need it, and `campaign.failed` is currently a no-op.
4. **Search and discovery** (Postgres FTS or Typesense/Meilisearch fed from the outbox), plus a cacheable, paginated, edge-cached public read API. Discovery is the growth surface and the first hot path.
5. **Server-side feature flags and entitlements** (an OpenFeature-compatible provider), enforced in the API and RLS, with per-cohort rollout and an audit trail. Required for regulated `layer2` and `postBeta` gating.
6. **An observability stack**: OpenTelemetry traces and metrics with correlation IDs from request to outbox to partner, SLO dashboards (settle lag, outbox age, webhook error rate), paging, and a worker heartbeat.
7. **Multi-environment and DR**: Supabase branching per preview, PITR on prod with documented RPO/RTO, nightly off-site export of ledger and audit to WORM storage, and a restore drill.
8. **Modular domain architecture in `apps/api`** (Funding, Commerce, Live, Compliance, Securities, Comms modules, each owning its tables, events and routes), plus a versioned OpenAPI contract generated from the `packages/shared` zod schemas. This lets the team scale and lets partner-facing APIs exist later.
9. **Owner-scoped ledger sub-accounts and balance snapshots.** These give per-backer and per-investor statements, Reg CF holdings and distributions, and multi-currency readiness without full-table sums.
10. **An admin and ops console backed by real queues** (KYC, campaign review, disputes, refunds, tranche evidence), with RBAC beyond `reviewer`/`admin` and four-eyes approval for money-moving actions like `verify_tranche`.
