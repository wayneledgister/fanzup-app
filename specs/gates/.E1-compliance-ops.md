# E1 Gate Review: Compliance / Ops Seat

Reviewer seat: COMPLIANCE/OPS (independent). Date: 2026-10-03.

## Findings

Scope read in full: `apps/api/src/**`, `supabase/migrations/*.sql`, `render.yaml`, `vercel.json`, `.github/workflows/{ci,db-deploy}.yml`, `docs/SETUP.md`, `docs/adr/ADR-002-hosting.md`, `docs/council/D1-open-decisions.md`, `specs/backlog.md`; web surveyed by grep (onboarding, settings, admin, identity queue).

Severity key: **[BLOCKER]** must be in place before real money or real users · **[CONCERN]** must be planned/tracked before Beta GA · **[NIT]** fix opportunistically.

Headline: the ledger core (balanced, append-only, idempotent, service-role-only) is better than most seed-stage money systems. Almost everything *around* it that a regulator, card network, auditor or on-call engineer would ask for does not exist yet: no reconciliation, no chargeback model, no post-funding refund path, no suspension switch, no tax/sanctions gates at payout, no production environment, no backups policy, no alerting.

### F1 [BLOCKER] The only real-money adapter has FanZuP holding and transmitting funds, contradicting "FanZuP never holds cash"
- **Target:** `apps/api/src/escrow/stripe-dev.ts:6-8, 15-25, 34-40`; `apps/api/src/escrow/provider.ts:1-8`; `render.yaml:20-21, 28-29`; `supabase/migrations/20261003000200_ledger_and_money.sql:13, 246-247`.
- **What:** `StripeDevEscrow` creates PaymentIntents on the *platform* account, funds settle to the platform's Stripe balance, then `transfers.create` pays the artist (separate charges and transfers). In live mode that is FanZuP receiving consumer funds and paying them to a third party: the fact pattern state money-transmitter statutes and FinCEN's MSB rule look at, and the opposite of the custody claim in `README.md` "Money-path rules". The `sk_test_` guard (`apps/api/src/env.ts:45-47`) is the only control, and `render.yaml` already ships `NODE_ENV=production` + `ESCROW_PROVIDER=stripe-dev`, so "production" currently means "test-mode Stripe".
- **Also:** `record_backing_refunded` debits `platform_funding` to make backers whole (`…ledger_and_money.sql:246`). That is FanZuP's own money entering the refund flow; in partner custody it requires a real pre-funded treasury transfer into the FBO/escrow account, which nothing models.
- **Why BLOCKER:** going live on this adapter (or a "temporary" live-key Stripe Connect variant) creates unlicensed-transmission exposure in most states and breaks the trust promise in Brand §7.3.
- **Required:** a written flow-of-funds memo from counsel (who is merchant of record, who is the payment settlement entity, whose account the money lands in, agent-of-payee analysis) signed off **before** any live key; a hard CI/runtime check that `stripe-dev` can never run against a production database; a treasury-funding procedure for absorbed refund fees.

### F2 [BLOCKER] No reconciliation against the partner/processor exists anywhere
- **Target:** `…ledger_and_money.sql:3-4` ("must reconcile to the partner's statements"), `:9` (`escrow_cash` "mirror of funds held"); absence of any recon table, job or route in `apps/api/src/**`.
- **What:** `escrow_cash` is only ever *derived* from our own postings. There is no ingestion of partner/processor balances or settlement reports, no per-campaign or per-transaction matching, no break table, no daily sign-off. `ledger_transactions.external_ref` (`:39`) is the only hook. Stripe fees are taken from the webhook-time balance transaction (`routes/webhooks.ts:21-27`) and never re-checked against payouts/adjustments.
- **Why BLOCKER:** a custody model where the platform claims a third party holds the money is only defensible if you can prove, daily, that the third party's statement equals your ledger. Without it, a missed webhook, a partner-side reversal or a fraudulent payout is invisible until a backer complains. Partners (North Capital, bank FBO via Modern Treasury) will contractually require it.
- **Required:** NFR-COMP-02 below: daily three-way recon (partner statement ↔ `ledger_balances` ↔ `backings`/`campaign_tranches` state), breaks queue with owner and ageing, and payouts auto-paused when unresolved breaks exceed threshold.

- **Also:** several states restrict use of the word "escrow" to licensed escrow agents or regulated entities. The web copy and README say "escrow" while the dev adapter is not escrow at all. Counsel should clear the term before launch marketing, and the copy should name the custodian.

### F3 [BLOCKER] Chargebacks and disputes have no model: no webhook, no ledger account, no hold on releases
- **Target:** `apps/api/src/routes/webhooks.ts:20-41` (handles only `payment_intent.succeeded`, `payment_intent.payment_failed`, `charge.refunded`); `docs/SETUP.md:161` (same three events subscribed); `…ledger_and_money.sql:18-21` (no `dispute_*` / `chargeback_loss` / `reserve` account kinds).
- **What:** a `charge.dispute.created` debits the platform balance (amount plus a dispute fee) with no ledger posting, so `escrow_cash` silently overstates what is held. Nothing stops `releaseVerifiedTranches` (`apps/api/src/money.ts:35-61`) paying the artist money that has already been clawed back. There is no evidence-submission workflow, no fraud-dispute vs. product-not-received split, and no rule for who carries the loss (artist payable, a reserve, or FanZuP).
- **Why BLOCKER:** reward crowdfunding has a structurally high "product not received" dispute rate because delivery comes months later. Card-network monitoring programs (dispute ratio thresholds) can terminate the processor relationship. Unmodelled clawbacks also break F2's reconciliation on day one.
- **Required:** NFR-COMP-04: dispute lifecycle ingestion, ledger postings (`dispute_hold`, `dispute_loss`, reserve), automatic pause of pending tranches for a campaign with open disputes above threshold, a disputes queue with deadlines, and a written loss-allocation policy in artist terms (rolling reserve or holdback on tranche 1).

### F4 [BLOCKER] Late, out-of-order or oversold captures strand captured money with no path back to the backer
- **Target:** `…ledger_and_money.sql:167-172` (`record_backing_captured` raises if the backing isn't `pending_payment`, the campaign isn't `live`, `now() >= ends_at`, or the perk `claimed` check constraint trips); `apps/api/src/routes/backings.ts:39` (sold-out check is advisory, outside any lock held through payment); `routes/webhooks.ts:21-28, 35-40`.
- **What:** a fan who opens checkout at 11:59 and pays at 12:01, a webhook delivered after `settle_campaign` ran, or two fans racing for the last limited perk all end the same way. Stripe captures the money, the SQL function raises, the webhook returns 500 (`app.ts:37` maps P0001/23514 to 409, which Stripe also treats as a failure), and Stripe retries for about 3 days and then gives up. The backing stays `pending_payment` forever, the money sits in the platform balance, nothing refunds it and nothing alerts anyone. The same applies to `charge.refunded` for a refund not initiated by settlement, such as a manual dashboard refund on a funded campaign: `record_backing_refunded` raises at `:241`.
- **Also:** there is no `processor_events` inbox table. Webhooks are processed inline with no stored copy, so there is nothing to replay, audit or reconcile against.
- **Why BLOCKER:** this is consumer money taken with no service delivered and no refund, which is exactly what consumer-protection enforcement targets.
- **Required:** NFR-COMP-05: store-then-process webhook inbox (unique on event id); any capture that can't be applied auto-refunds through the outbox and is audited; perk inventory reserved at backing creation with expiry (not claimed at capture); refunds outside the failed-campaign path have their own ledger function.

### F5 [BLOCKER] No remedy path after funding: no refund, no suspension, no fulfillment-failure handling
- **Target:** `…ledger_and_money.sql:241` (refunds only when `campaigns.status = 'failed'`); `supabase/migrations/20261003000100_core.sql:38-45` (`campaign_transitions`: no `live→suspended`, no `funded→refunding`, no `released→…`); `core.sql:19, 162-168` (`fulfillment_status` includes `Problem` but nothing consumes it); `…workflow_and_rls.sql:135, 197-199` (artists may set their own fulfillment `status` to `Delivered`); `perks.fulfill_by` (`core.sql:136`) is never checked by any job; `specs/backlog.md:5, 12` (disputes/refunds queues and fulfillment API are backlog only).
- **What:** once a campaign is `funded`, the only way money moves is toward the artist. Staff cannot freeze a live campaign that turns out to be fraudulent, cannot halt tranche 2, cannot refund a backer whose perk never arrived, and cannot claw back a released tranche. Delivery is self-attested by the party being paid.
- **Regulatory frame:** the FTC has treated undelivered crowdfunding rewards as unfair or deceptive (FTC v. Chevalier, 2015), and a state AG has done the same (Washington v. Altius / Asylum Playing Cards, 2014-15). The FTC Mail, Internet or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule (16 CFR 435) imposes ship-by, delay-notice and cancel-and-refund duties for merchandise orders. Whether it reaches reward pledges is a counsel question, but physical perks (`perk_kind = 'physical'`) look like merchandise orders, so we should design to it.
- **Why BLOCKER:** the product's core promise ("Fund the culture", escrow, milestone release) is a *protection* promise. Without a stop button and a post-funding refund path, that promise isn't real.
- **Required:** NFR-COMP-06/07: `suspended` status reachable from `live`/`funded` by staff only (dual control); release blocked while suspended; overdue `fulfill_by` triggers delay notices with a cancel-for-refund option to backers; a post-funding refund function funded from undisbursed `artist_payable` first, then reserve; delivery confirmation from the backer side (or carrier tracking) for physical perks before the final tranche.

### F6 [BLOCKER] Payouts have no tax-identity gate and no information-reporting design (1099-K / 1099-NEC, W-9/TIN)
- **Target:** `core.sql:59-77` (`artists`: `ein_verified`, `business_bank_verified` booleans but no `tax_profile_ref`, TIN-match status, W-9 certification date or backup-withholding flag); `apps/api/src/escrow/stripe-dev.ts:35` (only check before paying is "payout account exists"); `money.ts:35-61`; `core.sql:48-49` (good: TIN must live with a vendor by token, but there is no token column for it); D1 Card C (`docs/council/D1-open-decisions.md:118-126`) covers only the Layer 2 fan side.
- **What:** artists receive gross reward-crowdfunding proceeds. Someone (the processor, the escrow partner or FanZuP) is the payment settlement entity and must file 1099-K above the third-party-network threshold (reverted in 2025 to $20,000 and 200 transactions; counsel to confirm). FanZuP must file 1099-NEC for any service payments it makes itself (promotions, bounties, referral fees; the threshold was raised by 2025 legislation). Without a certified W-9 and a TIN match, backup withholding (24%) applies, and nothing in the release path can withhold.
- **Why BLOCKER:** the first live payout without a TIN on file creates a withholding liability FanZuP cannot cure retroactively.
- **Required:** NFR-COMP-08: W-9 (or W-8) collected via a tax vendor and TIN-matched before the first release; release blocked if missing or mismatched; PSE designation written into the partner contract; year-end 1099 generation with corrections; this applies to Layer 1 now, not only Layer 2.

### F7 [BLOCKER] Sanctions, AML and fraud controls are mock UI only; backers are never screened and payouts aren't gated
- **Target:** `apps/web/src/components/campaign/identityData.ts:1-70` (demo KYC/AML/OFAC queue, in-memory data); `core.sql:68-69` (`artists.identity_status`, `identity_ref`: the only identity fields; nothing on `profiles` for backers); `…workflow_and_rls.sql:33` (identity is checked only at `submit_campaign`, never at release); `money.ts:35-61` (no screening before `releaseToArtist`); no velocity, device or card-testing controls on `POST /backings` (`routes/backings.ts:13-61`).
- **What:** OFAC obligations apply to every US person, regardless of licensing. FanZuP pays artists and facilitates payments from backers, so both sides need screening at onboarding and re-screening against list updates, with a block-and-report path. Artist identity status is set once and never re-checked before money leaves. The backings endpoint has no rate limits, so it is an open card-testing oracle once a live processor is attached. Self-backing is blocked by exact user id only (`routes/backings.ts:35`); linked accounts, the D1 condition 3 concern, are not addressed at all.
- **Why BLOCKER:** paying a sanctioned person is strict liability. Card testing gets processor accounts closed within days.
- **Required:** NFR-COMP-09/10: artist KYC + OFAC/PEP screen before submission **and** a fresh screen before each release; backer screening at least at checkout name/country level by the processor or partner; daily list re-screen; per-user/IP/card velocity limits and processor Radar-equivalent rules; written AML/fraud risk assessment even if FanZuP is not itself an MSB (the partner will require it).

### F8 [CONCERN] Audit trail is append-only in principle but incomplete in coverage and context, with no retention mechanism
- **Target:** `…ledger_and_money.sql:79-88` (`audit_events`: actor, action, entity, data; no request id, IP, user agent, session, or before/after); unaudited state changes at `routes/webhooks.ts:30-33` (`payment_failed` update), `routes/backings.ts:41-44` (backing created), `…workflow_and_rls.sql:135, 197-199` (artist fulfillment updates), `core.sql:181-185` + `docs/SETUP.md:136-139` (staff/admin grants made by raw SQL with no audit row), `settle_campaign` and refund events written with `actor_id` null and no "system:worker" identity (`…ledger_and_money.sql:224, 256`).
- **Retention:** PRD 01 §10 requires 6+ years. `audit_events` lives in a mutable Postgres table that the `postgres` owner can `ALTER TABLE … DISABLE TRIGGER` or `TRUNCATE` (the append-only trigger is `BEFORE UPDATE OR DELETE` only, `:88`; truncate is not covered). There is no export to WORM storage, no hash chain and no documented retention schedule. The same applies to `ledger_*` (`:70-71`). Supabase backups (see F12) don't satisfy this: they rotate in days.
- **Required:** NFR-COMP-11/12: every state change on money, campaign, identity, staff or fulfillment entities emits an audit row with actor (user / staff / system:component), request id, source IP and before/after; `TRUNCATE` blocked by trigger; daily export of `audit_events` and `ledger_*` to object storage with object lock (≥ 7 years, which covers the 6-year rule plus tax-record periods); a hash chain or signed daily digest for tamper evidence.

### F9 [CONCERN] Account deletion promised in the UI cannot be executed against the schema, and no retention schedule exists
- **Target:** `apps/web/src/pages/settings/Settings.tsx:255-273` (promises deletion after `POLICY.account.deletionGraceDays`); `core.sql:51` (`profiles.id … on delete cascade` from `auth.users`); `core.sql:149` (`backings.backer_id … on delete restrict`), `core.sql:61` (`artists.owner_id … on delete restrict`); D1 Card B (`docs/council/D1-open-decisions.md:114`) leaves retention to counsel.
- **What:** deleting the auth user of anyone who ever backed or ran a campaign will fail on the FK, and if it were forced, ledger/audit history would lose its subject. That's correct for financial records, but the opposite of what the UI tells the user. CCPA/CPRA (if thresholds are met) and the comprehensive state privacy laws (VA, CO, CT, TX, OR and others) allow retention for legal obligations but require disclosure and minimization. There is no deletion job, no pseudonymization path, no data-subject export, no "do not sell/share" handling, and `profiles_read` (`…workflow_and_rls.sql:159`) makes every profile row, including `city`, publicly readable.
- **Required:** NFR-COMP-13/14: a retention schedule by data class; deletion = pseudonymize PII in `profiles`/`auth.users` and keep financial/audit records under legal hold; DSAR export and delete workflows with a 45-day SLA tracker; vendor-held PII (KYC, tax) deleted through vendor APIs; the copy changed to say what is kept and why.

### F10 [CONCERN] The 18+ age gate is client-side only and DOB is never persisted or enforced
- **Target:** `apps/web/src/pages/onboarding/Account.tsx:13, 40-54` (age computed in the browser; submit blocked locally); `core.sql:48-49` (DOB deliberately not stored); `…workflow_and_rls.sql` and `routes/backings.ts` (no age attestation is checked server-side); `handle_new_user` (`core.sql:194-200`) creates a profile for any sign-up.
- **What:** anyone calling Supabase Auth directly bypasses the gate. Holding all users to 18+ keeps FanZuP clear of COPPA (under 13) and of most state minor-privacy and card-acceptance problems, but only if it's enforced and evidenced. Note the tension with not storing DOB: we need at least an `age_attested_at` + method on the account, with full DOB kept by the KYC vendor for artists.
- **Required:** NFR-COMP-15: server-side attestation (signup through an API or Auth hook that rejects without it); `profiles.age_attested_at` non-null before backing; the KYC vendor's DOB check for artists; a takedown procedure for accounts reported as minors.

### F11 [BLOCKER] Terms, Privacy, artist agreement and backer disclosures don't exist; the UI collects consent to text that isn't written
- **Target:** `docs/CONSOLIDATION.md:45` (onboarding collects "terms consent"), `:140` (Terms and Privacy say "being finalized with counsel"); no `terms_version` / `consented_at` columns anywhere in `core.sql`; no artist agreement covering fulfillment duty, dispute loss allocation (F3), reserve, refund obligations (F5), tax (F6) or the absorbed-fee policy (`docs/SETUP.md:246-251`).
- **Why BLOCKER:** without signed artist terms FanZuP has no contractual basis to hold back tranches, claw back, or pass dispute losses to the artist. Without backer terms it has no disclosed refund policy, which is itself the first thing the FTC and state AGs look for. Consent must also be *recorded* (version + timestamp + IP) to be provable.
- **Required:** NFR-COMP-01/16: counsel-approved Terms of Use, Privacy Notice, Artist Campaign Agreement and Backer Pledge Terms before Beta; `legal_acceptances(user_id, doc, version, accepted_at, ip, user_agent)` table; re-acceptance on material version change; the campaign page shows the artist's delivery commitment and the refund policy at checkout.

### F12 [BLOCKER] A single (staging) Supabase project, with no production, backup, PITR or restore drill
- **Target:** `.github/workflows/db-deploy.yml:28` (hard-coded staging ref default `trreozwlpvgbcioatmrn`), `:47-49` (production job off until `PROD_DEPLOY_ENABLED`); `docs/SETUP.md:107-111` (instructions say create two projects; only staging exists, per the brief); `docs/SETUP.md:130-131` (seed users with a **published password** `FanzupDev123`, `:49`, are loaded into staging, which is the same project the hosted app uses today); `supabase/config.toml` (no backup settings; none expected, but nothing in docs either). `grep -ri "backup\|pitr\|restore"` across `docs/` returns nothing.
- **What:** there is no isolated production database. Real users on the current project would share it with seeded accounts whose passwords are public, one of which is a staff reviewer (`reviewer@fanzup.test`, `docs/SETUP.md:56`) who can approve campaigns. Supabase daily backups on paid plans are retained for a limited window; PITR is a paid add-on and must be turned on explicitly. A ledger with no tested restore is a ledger you can lose.
- **Required:** NFR-OPS-01/02/03: separate `fanzup-prod` project (Pro plan or above, PITR enabled), a separate Vercel production environment and Render worker bound to it, no seed ever; staging seeded accounts never granted staff in any shared project; quarterly restore drill into a scratch project with ledger invariants re-verified (sum of all entries = 0, per-transaction balance, recon to partner) and the result logged.

### F13 [BLOCKER] No alerting, no metrics and no dead-letter: failed refunds and payouts retry silently forever
- **Target:** `apps/api/src/money.ts:66-82` (`drainOutbox`: on error, `attempts + 1` and backoff capped at 3600 s; no max attempts, no dead-letter, no alert); `apps/api/src/worker.ts:27-31` (errors go to `console.error` only); `apps/api/src/app.ts:45-48` (`/health` checks only `select 1`); `render.yaml:6-14` (no health check or heartbeat for the worker); no Sentry/OTel/metrics dependency in `apps/api/package.json`.
- **What:** if the partner rejects a refund (closed card, partner outage, bad `payment_ref`), the row retries hourly indefinitely and no human ever learns of it. Backers were promised automatic refunds. If the Render worker crashes in a loop or stalls, campaigns stop settling and nothing pages. The API health check can't tell you the worker is dead, the outbox is backing up, or webhooks have stopped arriving.
- **Required:** NFR-OPS-04/05/06 and the operational baseline below: error tracking on API and worker; metrics for outbox depth, oldest-unprocessed age, attempts > N, campaigns past `ends_at` still `live`, webhook lag and recon breaks; worker heartbeat row checked by an external monitor; dead-letter after N attempts into a staffed queue with a runbook.

### F14 [CONCERN] Payout idempotency outlives the processor's idempotency window, so a post-payout DB failure can double-pay
- **Target:** `apps/api/src/money.ts:51-57` (calls `escrow.releaseToArtist` with key `release:<trancheId>` **then** `record_tranche_released`; failure between them leaves the tranche `verified`); `money.ts:79` (retry backoff grows to 1 h per attempt, so retries continue well past 24 h); `apps/api/src/escrow/stripe-dev.ts:36-39`.
- **What:** Stripe idempotency keys are pruned after about 24 hours. If `record_tranche_released` fails repeatedly (e.g. the `campaign.status <> 'funded'` check after a status change, or a DB outage) and the retry happens after the window, the processor creates a **second** transfer. The partner adapter will have its own window. There's no "payout intent" row written *before* calling out, so recon can't detect it either.
- **Required:** NFR-COMP-03: two-phase payouts: persist `payout_attempts(tranche_id, idem_key, status='initiated')` in the DB first; on retry, look up the partner transfer by idem key or `transfer_group` before creating; never call the partner for a tranche with an `initiated` attempt older than the window without a lookup; recon flags any partner transfer with no matching posting.

### F15 [CONCERN] Separation of duties: reviewers can approve their own campaigns, staff grants are unaudited SQL, and nobody verifies the verifier
- **Target:** `…workflow_and_rls.sql:52-64` (`review_campaign` checks `is_staff()` only, not that the reviewer is not the artist owner); `core.sql:181-185` + `docs/SETUP.md:134-139` (staff granted by hand-run SQL; the founder is both `admin` and could be an artist); `…ledger_and_money.sql:262-269` (`verify_tranche`: a single reviewer releases milestone money; no second approver, no evidence reference stored).
- **Why it matters:** milestone release *is* the investor-protection mechanism (Mechanism 05 §2.4). A single unchecked staff click releasing funds, with no evidence link, is the first thing a partner's due diligence and any later SOC 2 auditor will flag.
- **Required:** NFR-COMP-17: reviewer ≠ owner (enforced in SQL); tranche verification needs evidence id + two distinct staff approvals above a threshold; staff role changes via an audited function with reason; quarterly access review.

### F16 [CONCERN] The migration process can deploy to the live database without the guarded workflow, uses a floating CLI, and has no rollback/expand-contract discipline
- **Target:** `.github/workflows/db-deploy.yml:3-5` (normal path is Supabase's GitHub integration "Deploy to production on main", which bypasses the GitHub `production` environment's required reviewers described in `docs/SETUP.md:213`); `db-deploy.yml:43, 72` (`supabase/setup-cli` `version: latest`); `render.yaml:14-16` (worker auto-deploys on `supabase/migrations/**` changes, so code may ship before or after the schema it needs); no down-migrations or migration review checklist.
- **Required:** NFR-OPS-07: one migration path to production, gated by required reviewers; pinned CLI version; expand → deploy → contract for any change touching money tables; a pre-deploy `db diff` against prod and a tested backward-compatible window for API + worker; migrations touching `ledger_*`/`audit_events` require two approvers.

### F17 [CONCERN] No automated or manual WCAG 2.2 AA verification, and checkout will be third-party
- **Target:** `.github/workflows/ci.yml:14-25` (web job: typecheck, copy lint, build only; no axe/pa11y step); no accessibility tooling in any `package.json`; `docs/council/D1-open-decisions.md:153` (the 93-route sweep checks console errors and overflow only); Stripe Elements / partner checkout iframes are out of our control.
- **Why:** ADA Title III demand letters and suits against consumer web apps are routine, and the DOJ's 2024 Title II rule adopted WCAG 2.1 AA (2.2 AA is the current target and the stated one here). Payment and KYC flows are the highest-risk pages and are the ones delegated to vendors.
- **Required:** NFR-COMP-18: axe checks in the Playwright sweep (backlog item 5) failing CI on serious/critical; a manual screen-reader + keyboard pass on signup, back-a-campaign, KYC handoff and refund status before Beta; vendor VPATs for checkout and KYC; an accessibility statement with a contact route.

### F18 [CONCERN] Ops tooling is summaries over demo data: no real queues, no support system, no incident or status process
- **Target:** `apps/web/src/pages/admin/Queues.tsx:5, 13, 43-44` (Disputes and Refunds read static `DISPUTES`/`REFUNDS` from `identityData.ts`; the file header says full tooling "isn't specced yet"); `packages/shared/src/policy.ts:18` (SLA targets 2/1/3/1 business days with nothing to measure them); `specs/backlog.md:5, 7, 12`; `apps/web/src/pages/public/Support.tsx` (static page; no ticketing integration); no runbooks, on-call rota, incident severity scheme or status page anywhere in `docs/`.
- **What:** the SLAs exist only as numbers. With one founder and a part-time team, an on-call reality has to be designed, not assumed: who is paged when refunds dead-letter on a Saturday, and what the backer is told.
- **Required:** NFR-OPS-08/09/10: DB-backed queues (refunds, disputes, fulfillment problems, recon breaks, sanctions hits, DSARs) with assignment, SLA timers and audit; a support desk (e.g. a helpdesk with the user id and backing id attached); a severity matrix (SEV1 = money moved wrongly or can't move; SEV2 = degraded checkout/settlement; SEV3 = other); a public status page; written runbooks for the top five failure modes (worker down, webhook backlog, partner outage, recon break, suspected fraud campaign); and a blameless postmortem template.

### F19 [NIT] Small operability and hygiene gaps
- `apps/api/src/server.ts:13-29`: the misconfigured-app 503 lists missing variable *names* publicly on every route. That's fine on staging, but in production return a generic 503 and log the detail.
- `apps/api/src/app.ts:27`: the logger redacts auth and cookie headers but not `stripe-signature`, request bodies or email addresses; define a PII-redaction list in one place.
- `api_idempotency` (`…ledger_and_money.sql:104-111`): PRD 01a says ≥ 30 days, but there's no purge job, so the table grows forever and holds user ids indefinitely. Add a TTL sweep at 30–90 days.
- `outbox` (`:91-101`) has no retention either; processed rows should be archived with the audit export (F8).
- `render.yaml:10` (`plan: starter`): a single instance with no restart alerting. Acceptable for Beta only with F13's heartbeat in place.
- `apps/api/src/money.ts:24-32` settles campaigns one by one with no per-campaign error isolation, so one campaign that raises halts settlement of every campaign after it in that tick. Wrap each campaign and dead-letter it.

## Required obligations
Each item is phrased as a requirement. **Gate** marks what must be true before real money (RM) or before real users without money (RU).

| ID | Requirement | Gate | From |

## Operational baseline
The minimum I'd sign off for Beta with real money. These are targets for a small team, not aspirational enterprise numbers. Tighten them at GA.

### SLOs (28-day rolling)
| Service | SLI | Objective |
|---|---|---|
| Checkout (`POST /api/v1/backings` + provider confirm) | successful non-4xx responses / all | 99.9% |
| Checkout latency | p95 server time for `POST /backings` | < 800 ms |
| Public reads (`GET /campaigns*`) | availability | 99.9%; p95 < 400 ms |
| Settlement | campaigns settled within 15 min of `ends_at` | 99.5% |
| Failed-campaign refunds | refunds *initiated* within 1 h of settlement / *confirmed* within 5 business days | 99.9% / 99% |
| Tranche release | released within 1 business day of verification (excluding recon/sanctions holds) | 99% |
| Webhook processing | events applied within 5 min of receipt | 99.9% |
| Reconciliation | daily recon completed by 10:00 ET with zero unexplained breaks > 1 business day | 100% (any miss is a SEV2) |
| Money correctness | ledger invariant violations, double payouts, unrefunded captures | **0** (any occurrence is a SEV1) |

### Recovery
- **RPO:** ≤ 5 minutes for the production database (PITR); ≤ 24 h for off-platform logical backups; 0 for audit/ledger exports once written (object lock).
- **RTO:** ≤ 4 h for API + DB; ≤ 1 h for the worker (redeployable anywhere: it's stateless); checkout may be disabled with a feature flag while money paths recover.
- **Drills:** quarterly restore drill (NFR-OPS-02); semi-annual "custodian outage" game day exercising pause-releases, a status page update and backer comms.
- **Single points of failure to document:** one Supabase region (us-east-1), one Render worker instance, one processor/custodian. Each needs a written degrade mode: a read-only site, a queued settlement, or "pledges paused".

### Alerting (page = on-call phone; ticket = next business day)
| Signal | Threshold | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Ledger invariant/recon break, double-payout detector, capture without backing | any | **Page SEV1**, auto-pause releases |
| Dead-lettered refund or payout | any | Page |
| Worker heartbeat missing | > 10 min | Page |
| Campaign `live` past `ends_at` | > 30 min | Page |
| Outbox oldest unprocessed | > 15 min | Page; > 5 min ticket |
| Webhook signature failures or 5xx | > 5 in 10 min | Page |
| Checkout 5xx rate | > 2% over 5 min | Page |
| Dispute ratio per campaign / platform | > 0.5% / > 0.65% of monthly transactions | Ticket / page (network monitoring programs start near 0.9%) |
| Sanctions hit, velocity-limit trips | any hit / spike > 3× baseline | Ticket to the compliance queue / page on spike |
| Queue SLA breach (refunds 1 bd, disputes 3 bd, identity 1 bd) | at 75% of SLA | Ticket to queue owner |
| Error-budget burn | 2% in 1 h or 5% in 6 h | Page |
| Supabase DB CPU / connections | > 80% for 10 min | Ticket |

### Process minimums
- **On-call:** named primary and secondary every week, even if the secondary is a contractor on retainer; paging via a real tool, not email.
- **Incidents:** SEV1 = money moved wrongly, can't move, or data exposed; SEV2 = checkout or settlement degraded; SEV3 = everything else. SEV1/2 get a status page update within 30 min and a postmortem within 5 business days. Data exposure starts the breach-notification clock (state laws; counsel on retainer).
- **Change management:** money-path PRs (ledger SQL, `money.ts`, escrow adapters, webhooks) need two reviewers and a passing invariant test suite; production deploys are frozen during settlement windows of large campaigns.
- **Evidence for partners and later SOC 2:** keep the recon sign-offs, access reviews, restore-drill logs and postmortems in one folder from day one. The custodian's due diligence will ask for them.

The ledger core is sound. What's missing is the regulated perimeter around it: custody memo, reconciliation, disputes, tax/sanctions gates, recorded consent, and a production environment with backups and alerting.
